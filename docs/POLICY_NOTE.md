## Why I am writing

Crop insurance under PMFBY is moving towards technology-based yield estimation (YES-TECH),
with satellite data carrying part of the weight. This season I followed seven rain-fed fields, two of
them my family's, in Sawardoh and Karanja Ghadge (0.22 to 0.98 ha; cotton, tur and soyabean) from sowing in late
June, with walked GPS boundaries, a weekly geotagged photo of each field and every Sentinel-2
pass over them. I am sharing what I found because it bears on how losses on small fields are
assessed in a season like this one.

## What I found

1. **The fields were not visible from orbit for most of the growing season.** Sentinel-2 passed
   25 or 26 times after sowing. Each field was clear on only 3 to 5 of those passes. Between
   14 July and mid-September there was no usable look at any field: 65 days for the Karanja
   Ghadge fields, 70 to 75 days for the Sawardoh fields. The crop grew from about NDVI 0.3 to
   0.7 or 0.8 inside that gap without a single observation.
2. **Two villages 8 km apart were seen on different days.** On 17 September the Karanja Ghadge
   fields were clear and the Sawardoh fields were not. Farmers in one taluka would be assessed
   on different evidence.
3. **The smallest field is measured worst.** The 0.22 ha field has 10 whole 10 m pixels inside
   its boundary and none at 20 m. Radar, which sees through cloud, gave a high vegetation
   reading there six days after sowing, on bare ground.
4. **Rainfall depends on the dataset.** Two public rainfall products agree on July (about 350 to
   380 mm) and differ by about 60 mm for August (about 100 against 160 mm).
5. **Insurance cover on the ground.** On one field only the young orange trees were insured;
   the cotton in the same field was not.

## What may help, at little cost

- For small rain-fed fields, keep crop-cutting experiments and field evidence weighted
  heavily wherever the satellite record has a long gap in the growing weeks.
- Tell farmers, through the e-Peek Pahani and insurance awareness work, that dated photos of
  their own crop are the evidence a satellite may not have for the monsoon weeks.
- The boundaries, the pass-by-pass audit and the scripts are public and can be rerun for
  other fields: github.com/sakshimaske303-commits/ROOTS-AND-REFLECTANCE.

## Limits

Seven fields, two villages, one season. This is a measured case, not a survey of the taluka. I
have not seen how YES-TECH is applied in Wardha district, so I make no claim about how that
system performed. The figures describe the observation problem any such system has to work
around on fields of this size.
